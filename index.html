The idea

What is Project 157?

Have you had the same dreams and goals since Prince Charles married Lady Di? Imagine how soon you could make progress if you could untangle the web in your head and get deep clarity on your first step.

Ross Edgley swam around the entire coast of Britain. In 157 days! On that journey he endured hundreds of jellyfish stings, a rotting tongue from the salt water, a suspected torn shoulder and an open neck wound.

I love the idea of looking at what we could achieve in that same timeframe. Not what it looks like for extreme athletes or people with “a bias for action”. What could it look like for us: the deep feelers and thinkers, who want to get into action but feel stuck in thinking about it?

I'm not suggesting we take on something as ridiculously gruelling, or get involved in a toxic productivity push. But let's take the idea that seemingly impossible things can be completed in 157 days, and see how we can apply it to our own lives: what we can achieve, and how that changes us and our perception of ourselves along the way.

That's Project 157: 157 days of focusing on your goal, so that you're more actively involved in your own life. It isn't really about getting to any particular place (although if that happens, of course that's wonderful). It's about keeping a promise to yourself that you will show up for yourself and for your dreams, and that you will do that consistently.

Who this is for

The woman I am talking to

I have a particular type of woman in mind. She is quiet, introvert, and sensitive – a deep feeler and a deep thinker. She will have at least one goal that's been on her mind for a long time; definitely months, probably years.

But her brain won't co-operate. There's so much going on, it feels like it's got 57 tabs open at the same time. There's a lot of confusion and indecision as well as some self-doubt or worry about what other people think. Having said that, deep down she knows she's smart and capable. She just can't seem to get going. She sees other women getting further ahead, getting stuff done, reaching their goals and making things happen. It feels like time is running out and she's lagging behind.

Like a fat pigeon trying to catch Usain Bolt in the 100 metres.

She has lots going on in her life: work, family, maybe caring responsibilities, lots of things to juggle. She's carrying a high mental load and doing more than her fair share of emotional labour too. There's a huge amount of mental activity that isn't accompanied by outside progress on the things that really matter to her. She is way down on her own list of priorities.

And more than all of this… She's searching.

Searching for a different kind of space, a space where she doesn't have to earn her place, where she's not interrupted, spoken over, ignored, where her nervous system isn't assaulted by noise and other people's opinions, and she's not told what she should do, and she's certainly not told how quickly she should do it. She's looking for a place where she can just be herself and explore what it is she wants in her own time and her own way, without any expectations being put on her of what that should look like.

And she's ready to lay down the doubt and confusion and take her first steps. Ready to feel vulnerable but also optimistic. Ready to experience failure in order to embrace success. Ready to make space for herself in her own life, and really see what she's capable of.

The Invitation

Project 157 is going to be a very different kind of coaching programme. It will give women 157 days of one-to-one support, slicing right through the doubt and confusion without any unnecessary frameworks, practices or exercises. But right now, there are still some wrinkles that need ironing out.

So, I'm starting with a six-week sprint. I'll explore exactly how this programme will come together, whilst at the same time participants will experience the power of focus and clarity without a long-term commitment.

If you join, you'll have support throughout, so you're not on your own. And the support comes in two parallel tracks. Firstly, it's having somebody by your side as you walk through what will undoubtedly be some difficult times. Because if it was easy, you'd have done it yourself by now. And secondly, somebody to make a clearing for you so you can step out from underneath the ruminating and mental looping and finally settle on what's true for you underneath all that thinking.

The Philosophy

“Step 10 can't be determined from step one.”

I wanted to take this opportunity to just briefly say a little bit about why I've created this programme in this way.

It was such a paradox. On the one hand, I wanted to stand up and be myself and create something that I didn't see many, if any, other coaches creating. And at the same time, I wanted somebody to show me how to do that, to say that everything was going to be OK, and that this is the seven-step plan to follow. I had so much doubt going on in my head, such an active and activated inner critic.

This year I found some coaching, for the first time in a very long time, that was really suited to who I am, how I think and the kind of support that I needed. It really opened up the idea to me that all this time I'd been trying to satisfy all the conditions my inner critic could come up with, in the false hope that one day all the conditions would be satisfied and I could finally get going. And I just saw so clearly that that was never going to happen. My inner critic was always going to offer me up reasons not to do something.

The way to sidestep that was not from my logical thinking brain, but coming at it from something that felt truer to who I am and was more heartfelt in every sense of the word. It came more from my heart than my head. And when I had access to a space that allowed me to connect to that heartfelt position, so much became clearer to me: who I am as a coach, who I am as a woman, who I want to work with, how I want to work with them and what I want to say to them. None of it needed anybody else to rubber-stamp it. None of it needed anybody to say, right, these are the seven steps you follow.

Along with the clarity of what I wanted to do came the certainty of how I wanted to do it, the resilience to cope with the inevitable rise and fall of that journey, and the courage to risk and to try and to fall, but to pick myself up and keep going again. All without outsourcing my authority, all while being convinced in myself that this is my path to follow, and that nothing is more important than stepping onto that path. No matter how circuitous the journey is, it's not even really the destination that matters, but taking my first step, and after that taking every next step that comes to mind.

And letting go of the need to be like a chess grandmaster, thinking 5, 7, 10, 15 steps ahead. There's a freedom in not needing to do the mental gymnastics of trying to work out what I need to do next week or next month, how I'm going to cope if this thing happens or that thing happens, or what needs to happen if such an event occurs. There's such a liberation in not trying to think so many steps ahead, and a corresponding release of energy when I'm not trying to micromanage every single thing.

Because honestly, step 10 can't be determined from step one. There's no earthly way that where I am now and who I am now could even solve the answer to step 10. The only person who knows what step 10 needs to be is the step 9 version of me. It's only when I've been through all those iterations, taken all those actions and made all those decisions that step 10 could possibly even manifest itself to me. So it's futile trying to do tomorrow's job today.

And I am just so energised and excited to bring this kind of support to women who may be looking for that same kind of understanding and support.
